We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Twin Turbo: calibrated hybrid edge
intelligence and particle-filter digital twins for autonomous turbofan
maintenance decisions", for consideration as a research article in the
International Journal of Information Technology.

The paper addresses a practical systems question: how can an inexpensive model
running on every engine cycle work together with a more detailed, per-engine
"digital twin" so that maintenance decisions are both safe and economical,
without consulting the expensive model all the time? Our framework runs a small
tree-based model at the edge on every cycle and contacts the engine's digital
twin only when one of three simple triggers fires. Each model reports a range
that is checked statistically to contain the true remaining life about 90% of
the time, and the system acts on the more cautious of the two ranges.

We evaluate the system on the decisions it makes, not only on prediction error.
Using the public NASA C-MAPSS turbofan dataset, we replay 709 held-out engines
cycle by cycle from first use to failure and compare our policy against nine
alternatives, including fixed-age replacement and deep-learning baselines. The
main findings are:

  - No engine failed under the hybrid policy, at any grounding threshold we
    tested and on all four sub-datasets, including with no safety margin at
    all. Every other policy we evaluated needed a dataset-specific safety
    margin to avoid failures.
  - The digital twin was consulted on only about one cycle in four (21-28%),
    so most of the time the system relies on the cheap edge model alone.
  - Long-run maintenance cost fell by 34-47% compared with the best fixed-age
    replacement schedule.
  - The twin's uncertainty ranges were well calibrated (86-94% coverage of a
    90% target), whereas a common deep-learning alternative (LSTM with
    Monte-Carlo dropout) covered only 43-54%.

We believe the work fits the journal's scope in applied computing and
intelligent systems: it combines edge computing, machine learning and
decision-making in one end-to-end pipeline, and it is fully reproducible.
The complete source code, experiment scripts and cached results are publicly
available at https://github.com/Arc-001/twin_turbo, and every table and figure
in the paper can be regenerated from them.
