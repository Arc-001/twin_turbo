Dear Editor,
Expert Systems with Applications

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Twin Turbo: An intelligent maintenance
decision system combining calibrated edge models and particle-filter digital
twins for turbofan engines", for consideration as a full-length article in
Expert Systems with Applications.

The paper presents an intelligent decision-support system for aircraft-engine
maintenance that combines two sources of knowledge. A lightweight model running
on the engine evaluates every flight cycle. A per-engine digital twin, which
encodes fleet-level knowledge of how engines degrade and updates it with that
engine's own evidence, is consulted only when one of three simple triggers
fires. Both tiers report calibrated uncertainty, and the system acts on the
more cautious of the two, so that it knows how far to trust its own
conclusions before deciding to ground an engine.

Our central claim concerns safety and efficiency rather than prediction
accuracy alone. We evaluate the system on the decisions it makes by replaying
709 held-out engines from the public NASA C-MAPSS dataset cycle by cycle from
first use to failure, and compare it against fixed-age replacement, deep-learning
baselines and single-model alternatives:

  - No engine failed under our system, at any grounding threshold we tested and
    on all four sub-datasets, including with no safety margin. Every other
    policy evaluated needed a dataset-specific margin to avoid failures.
  - The expensive digital twin was consulted on only about one cycle in four
    (21-28%), so most decisions rely on the inexpensive edge model alone.
  - Long-run maintenance cost fell by 34-47% relative to the best fixed-age
    replacement schedule.
  - The twin's uncertainty ranges were well calibrated (86-94% coverage of a
    90% target), whereas Monte-Carlo dropout on an LSTM covered only 43-54%.

We believe this work fits the journal's focus on intelligent systems applied
to real decision problems: it shows how knowledge-based and data-driven
components can be combined into an autonomous decision system whose
confidence can be verified, and it discusses the practical implications for
deployment under limited connectivity. All code, experiment scripts and cached
results are publicly available at https://github.com/Arc-001/twin_turbo, and
every table and figure can be regenerated from them.
